This chapter presents the methodological framework of the study. It first
defines MPVRP-CC, then develops a mixed-integer linear formulation and an
approach based on constraint programming and sequence variables. The final
section presents the experimental protocol for comparing the approaches and
assessing the effect of changeover costs.

\section{Problem definition}\label{sec:problem-definition}
\subsection{Description}
The Multi-Product Vehicle Routing Problem with Changeover Costs can be defined
as the problem of organizing deliveries of several products from a set of
depots to a set of stations using a fleet of capacitated vehicles. Each station
requires specified quantities of one or more products, while depots hold
limited stocks. A vehicle may perform several trips during the planning period.

Each trip begins with the loading of one product at a depot. The vehicle then
visits stations requiring that product and delivers its entire load before
loading again. The following trip may begin at a different depot. Each used
vehicle departs from its home garage and returns there after its final delivery.

Successive loads may require preparation. The associated changeover cost
depends on both the previous product and the next product. The initial product
state of a vehicle determines the preparation required before its first load.

The planning decisions concern delivery quantities, loading sources, vehicle
assignments, and the order of operations. The aim is to satisfy all demands
while minimizing travel and changeover costs. A shorter route is therefore
not necessarily the least expensive plan.

\subsection{Requests, supply nodes, and notation}
A \textbf{request} is a station--product pair with positive demand. A
\textbf{supply node} is a depot--product pair with positive available stock.
Several requests may share one station, and several supply nodes may share
one depot. These logical nodes retain their physical locations. A supply node
may support several loading operations, provided its stock is sufficient.

Quantities use a common load unit. Travel and changeover costs use a common
cost scale so that they can be added. The changeover matrix may be asymmetric:
preparing a vehicle for $p'$ after $p$ need not cost the same as the reverse
transition. Same-product transitions retain the specified value $h_{pp}$.

\subsection{Problem constraints}
The problem is subject to the following constraints.
\begin{enumerate}
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\alph{enumi}.}
\item \textbf{Demand satisfaction.} Each request receives exactly $d_r$ units.
\item \textbf{Stock availability.} Total withdrawals from supply node $u$ do
not exceed $B_u$.
\item \textbf{Vehicle capacity.} The quantity carried during a trip does not
exceed the capacity of the vehicle performing it.
\item \textbf{Product consistency.} A trip carries one product and serves
only requests for that product.
\item \textbf{Load balance.} The entire load is delivered during the trip;
no remaining quantity is transferred to the next load.
\item \textbf{Route continuity.} Loading and delivery operations form a
connected route, without disconnected customer cycles.
\item \textbf{Departure and return.} Each used vehicle starts and ends at its
own garage. Vehicles may remain unused.
\item \textbf{Split deliveries and visit limits.} Several vehicles may share
a request's demand, but each vehicle visits that request at most once. This
limit concerns a station--product pair, not all products required at a station.
\end{enumerate}

Vehicles may perform several trips. Time windows, service durations, and
working-time limits are outside the present setting. Preparation is represented
by a cost rather than by a duration or a forbidden product transition.

\subsection{Objective function}
Suppose vehicle $k$ performs $m_k>0$ trips with products
$(p_{k1},\ldots,p_{km_k})$. Its changeover cost is
\[
C_k^{\mathrm{change}}=h_{b_kp_{k1}}+
\sum_{t=1}^{m_k-1}h_{p_{kt}p_{k,t+1}}.
\]
The first term accounts for the initial product state. An unused vehicle
incurs zero cost. No final cleaning charge is added after the last trip.

The objective is
\begin{equation}
\min C_{\mathrm{total}}=C_{\mathrm{travel}}+
\sum_{k\in K}C_k^{\mathrm{change}}.
\label{eq:common-objective}
\end{equation}
Travel includes departure from the garage, delivery paths, connections between
trips, and garage return. For example, travel costing 28 units and changeovers
costing 6 and 8 units give a total of $28+6+8=42$ units.

\section{MILP model}\label{sec:milp-model}
\subsection{Trip positions and routing graph}
The formulation represents each vehicle's plan through ordered trip positions
$T=\{1,\ldots,H\}$. The parameter $H$ is the maximum number of trips available
to each vehicle. It may be chosen explicitly; otherwise, a default value is
\begin{equation}
H=\max\left\{
\left\lceil\frac{\sum_{r\in R}d_r}{\sum_{k\in K}Q_k}\right\rceil+1,
|P|\right\}.
\label{eq:trip-horizon}
\end{equation}

The demand-to-capacity ratio estimates the number of loading rounds needed
if the entire fleet were used at full capacity. Rounding up and adding one
provides an extra round. The second term leaves at least as many positions
as there are products, allowing room for product-separated trips.

This is a practical default, not a sufficient bound proved to preserve an
unrestricted optimum. Any chosen value must satisfy the necessary condition
$H\sum_kQ_k\geq\sum_rd_r$. Product demands, stock distribution, and visit
limits may still require a larger value. Optimality conclusions therefore
apply to the selected limit $H$.

The routing graph contains three arc sets:
\begin{align}
A_{UR}&=\{(u,r)\in U\times R:p_u=p_r\},\label{eq:arcs-ur}\\
A_{RR}&=\{(r,r')\in R\times R:r\neq r',\ p_r=p_{r'}\},\label{eq:arcs-rr}\\
A_{RU}&=R\times U.\label{eq:arcs-ru}
\end{align}
\begin{description}
\item[$A_{UR}$.] Arcs from a loading source to the first request of a trip.
Both nodes concern the same product.
\item[$A_{RR}$.] Arcs joining distinct requests during a trip. Both requests
concern the product being transported.
\item[$A_{RU}$.] Arcs from the last request of a trip to the loading source
of the next trip, which may concern another product.
\end{description}

Let $A=A_{UR}\cup A_{RR}\cup A_{RU}$ and $n=|R|$. Define
$A^+(i)=\{j:(i,j)\in A\}$ and $A^-(i)=\{j:(j,i)\in A\}$.
Garage departure and return are represented separately.

\subsection{Decision variables}

$e_{ktr}$ & $\{0,1\}$ & Return home occurs from request $r$ after trip $t$ \\

The variable $w$ is defined only for $t<H$. Set $a_{k,H+1}=0$ and
$s_{k,H+1,u}=0$ as boundary constants. Write $L_{kt}=\sum_ry_{ktr}$ for the
total delivery quantity of a trip; it is shorthand, not an additional variable.

\subsection{Constraints}
\subsubsection{Trip activation and loading source}
For $k\in K$, $t\in T$, and $p\in P$,
\begin{align}
\sum_{u\in U}s_{ktu}&=a_{kt},\label{eq:milp-start}\\
z_{ktp}&=\sum_{u\in U:p_u=p}s_{ktu},\label{eq:milp-product}\\
a_{k,t+1}&\leq a_{kt} &&(t<H),\label{eq:milp-prefix}\\
f_{kt}&=a_{kt}-a_{k,t+1}.\label{eq:milp-last}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:milp-start} selects one loading source for each active trip
and none for an inactive position. Equation~\eqref{eq:milp-product} fixes the
product through that source. Constraint~\eqref{eq:milp-prefix} places active
trips consecutively from the first position. Equation~\eqref{eq:milp-last}
identifies the final trip, after which the vehicle returns home.

\subsubsection{Route continuity}
For each vehicle $k$ and trip $t$,
\begin{align}
\sum_{j\in A^+(u)}x_{ktuj}&=s_{ktu} &&(u\in U),\label{eq:milp-leave}\\
\sum_{i\in A^-(u)}x_{ktiu}&=s_{k,t+1,u} &&(u\in U),\label{eq:milp-next}\\
\sum_{r\in R}e_{ktr}&=f_{kt},\label{eq:milp-home}\\
\sum_{i\in A^-(r)}x_{ktir}&=v_{ktr} &&(r\in R),\label{eq:milp-in}\\
\sum_{j\in A^+(r)}x_{ktrj}+e_{ktr}&=v_{ktr} &&(r\in R),\label{eq:milp-out}\\
v_{ktr}&\leq z_{kt,p_r} &&(r\in R).\label{eq:milp-compatible}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:milp-leave} starts a trip at its selected source.
Equation~\eqref{eq:milp-next} connects its final request to the source of the
following trip. Equation~\eqref{eq:milp-home} instead selects one garage return
when the trip is the last one.

Equations~\eqref{eq:milp-in}--\eqref{eq:milp-out} give each visited request
one incoming and one outgoing connection, including garage return as an
alternative. Constraint~\eqref{eq:milp-compatible} permits service only when
the trip carries the requested product.

\subsubsection{Demand and visit limits}
\begin{align}
\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{t\in T}y_{ktr}&=d_r &&(r\in R),\label{eq:milp-demand}\\
\sum_{t\in T}v_{ktr}&\leq1 &&(k\in K,r\in R),\label{eq:milp-visit-limit}\\
0\leq y_{ktr}&\leq d_rv_{ktr} &&(k\in K,t\in T,r\in R).
\label{eq:quantity-visit}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:milp-demand} satisfies demand exactly.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:milp-visit-limit} permits splitting across vehicles but
forbids repeated visits by one vehicle to the same request.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:quantity-visit} permits deliveries only at selected visits.
It allows $y_{ktr}=0$ when $v_{ktr}=1$: a selected visit need not carry a
positive quantity in this formulation. Requiring at least one unit at every
visit would additionally require $y_{ktr}\geq v_{ktr}$.

\subsubsection{Capacity and stock}
For $k\in K$, $t\in T$, and $u\in U$,
\begin{align}
L_{kt}&\leq Q_ka_{kt},\label{eq:milp-capacity}\\
l_{ktu}&\leq Q_ks_{ktu},\label{eq:milp-load-source}\\
l_{ktu}&\leq L_{kt},\label{eq:milp-load-upper}\\
l_{ktu}&\geq L_{kt}-Q_k(1-s_{ktu}),\label{eq:milp-load-lower}\\
\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{t\in T}l_{ktu}&\leq B_u &&(u\in U).\label{eq:milp-stock}
\end{align}
Constraint~\eqref{eq:milp-capacity} limits the trip quantity to vehicle
capacity. Constraints~\eqref{eq:milp-load-source}--\eqref{eq:milp-load-lower}
set the load to $L_{kt}$ at the chosen source and zero elsewhere. The coefficient
$Q_k$ is sufficient because it already bounds the trip quantity.
Constraint~\eqref{eq:milp-stock} limits total withdrawals across all trips.

\subsubsection{Subtour elimination}
For $k\in K$ and $t\in T$,
\begin{align}
v_{ktr}\leq o_{ktr}&\leq nv_{ktr} &&(r\in R),\label{eq:milp-order}\\
o_{ktr}-o_{ktr'}+nx_{ktrr'}&\leq n-1
&&((r,r')\in A_{RR}).\label{eq:milp-mtz}
\end{align}
Constraint~\eqref{eq:milp-order} assigns positive positions only to visited
requests. Constraint~\eqref{eq:milp-mtz} increases the position along each
selected request-to-request arc. A disconnected cycle would require positions
to increase back to their starting value, which is impossible. These constraints
follow the Miller--Tucker--Zemlin ordering principle~\cite{miller1960integer}.

\subsubsection{Product transitions}
For $k\in K$, $t<H$, and $p,p'\in P$,
\begin{align}
w_{ktpp'}&\leq z_{ktp},\label{eq:milp-change-first}\\
w_{ktpp'}&\leq z_{k,t+1,p'},\label{eq:milp-change-second}\\
w_{ktpp'}&\geq z_{ktp}+z_{k,t+1,p'}-1.\label{eq:milp-change-both}
\end{align}
Constraints~\eqref{eq:milp-change-first}--\eqref{eq:milp-change-both} set
$w_{ktpp'}$ to one exactly when the current trip carries $p$ and the following
trip carries $p'$. This makes the corresponding changeover charge linear.

\subsection{Objective function}
\begin{align}
\min\quad
&\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{(i,j)\in A}c_{ij}x_{ktij}
+\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{u\in U}c_{g_ku}s_{k1u}\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{r\in R}c_{rg_k}e_{ktr}
+\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{p\in P}h_{b_kp}z_{k1p}\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{t=1}^{H-1}\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{p'\in P}
 h_{pp'}w_{ktpp'}.
\label{eq:milp-objective}
\end{align}
The five terms represent travel within and between trips, garage departure,
garage return, preparation before the first load, and changeovers between
successive loads. Every movement and transition is counted once.

\section{Proposed approach}\label{sec:proposed-approach}
\subsection{Overview}
The proposed approach separates the preparation of delivery trips from their
assignment and sequencing. Requests are divided into delivery pieces, associated
with sources, and grouped into mini-trips. Each mini-trip receives an internal
visit order. CP then chooses its vehicle and position in that vehicle's route.

A \textbf{delivery piece} is a positive portion of a request's demand supplied
by one source. A \textbf{mini-trip} consists of a loading operation followed
by an ordered list of compatible pieces. Its path ends at its final delivery;
the connection to the next operation is determined during sequencing.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Allocation of planning decisions}\label{tab:decision-allocation}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule Decision & Preliminary construction & CP optimization \\ \midrule
Quantities & Selects delivery pieces & Keeps quantities fixed \\
Supply nodes & Selects loading sources & Keeps sources fixed \\
Mini-trip composition & Groups compatible pieces & Keeps groups fixed \\
Internal order & Orders deliveries & Keeps internal paths fixed \\
Vehicle assignment & Leaves it open & Selects eligible vehicles \\
Mini-trip succession & Leaves it open & Orders trips on each vehicle \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The separation reduces the decisions explored together, but restricts the
solution space. CP does not reconsider the preliminary quantities, sources,
groupings, or paths. Its optimality conclusions concern the resulting mini-trips.

\subsection{Construction of delivery pieces}
Let $B_u^{\mathrm{rem}}$ be remaining stock and $d^{\mathrm{rem}}$ the remaining
demand of the current request. Algorithm~\ref{alg:pieces} handles large demands
first and favors the source supplying the largest possible piece. Distance
breaks quantity ties. Capacities guide splitting without assigning pieces to
particular vehicles.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\SetAlgoLined
\KwData{Requests $R$, sources $U$, demands $d_r$, stocks $B_u$, capacities $Q_k$, costs $c$}
\KwResult{Delivery pieces $E$, or construction failure}
$E\gets\varnothing$; $B_u^{\mathrm{rem}}\gets B_u$ for all $u\in U$\;
Order requests by nonincreasing demand\;
Form list $L$ of vehicle capacities in nonincreasing order\;
\ForEach{request $r$ in this order}{
 $d^{\mathrm{rem}}\gets d_r$\;
 \ForEach{capacity $Q$ in $L$, while $d^{\mathrm{rem}}>0$}{
  $U_r\gets\{u\in U:p_u=p_r,\ B_u^{\mathrm{rem}}>0\}$\;
  \If{$U_r=\varnothing$}{\Return{construction failure}\;}
  $v_u\gets\min\{d^{\mathrm{rem}},Q,B_u^{\mathrm{rem}}\}$ for $u\in U_r$\;
  Select $u^*$ maximizing $v_u$, then minimizing $c_{ur}$\;
  Add $(r,u^*,v_{u^*})$ to $E$\;
  $d^{\mathrm{rem}}\gets d^{\mathrm{rem}}-v_{u^*}$\;
  $B_{u^*}^{\mathrm{rem}}\gets B_{u^*}^{\mathrm{rem}}-v_{u^*}$\;
 }
 \If{$d^{\mathrm{rem}}>0$}{\Return{construction failure}\;}
}
\Return{$E$}\;
\caption{Demand splitting and supply-node selection}\label{alg:pieces}
\end{algorithm}

At most $|K|$ pieces are created for one request. Failure means that this
construction did not find a suitable split, not that the original problem is
infeasible. Earlier allocations are not reconsidered.

\subsection{Grouping and ordering deliveries}
Write $r_e,u_e,v_e$ for the request, source, and quantity of piece $e$.
For group $j$, let $u_j,q_j,R_j$ denote its source, accumulated quantity, and
represented requests. Set $Q_{\min}=\min_kQ_k$.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\SetAlgoLined
\KwData{Pieces $E$ and threshold $Q_{\min}$}
\KwResult{Groups $J$ of compatible delivery pieces}
$J\gets\varnothing$\;
Order pieces by nonincreasing quantity\;
\ForEach{piece $e$ in this order}{
 $F\gets\{j\in J:u_j=u_e,\ r_e\notin R_j,\ q_j+v_e\leq Q_{\min}\}$\;
 \eIf{$F\neq\varnothing$}{
  Select $j\in F$ minimizing $Q_{\min}-q_j-v_e$\;
 }{
  Create group $j$ with source $u_e$, quantity 0 and no requests\;
  Add $j$ to $J$\;
 }
 Add $e$ to $j$; update $q_j$ and $R_j$\;
}
\Return{$J$}\;
\caption{Best-fit grouping of delivery pieces}\label{alg:grouping}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:grouping} favors the eligible group with least residual
space. A piece exceeding $Q_{\min}$ forms a singleton group, which requires
a sufficiently large vehicle. A group cannot contain two pieces of one request.

For requests $(r_1,\ldots,r_m)$ ordered after source $u$, define
\[
I(u;r_1,\ldots,r_m)=c_{ur_1}+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}c_{r_ir_{i+1}}.
\]
This open-path cost contains no return leg. Algorithm~\ref{alg:internal-routing}
uses nearest neighbor followed by best-improvement subsequence reversals.
Each candidate is evaluated through its full path cost, so symmetry is not
required.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\SetAlgoLined
\KwData{Group $j$ with source $u_j$ and pieces $E_j$}
\KwResult{An ordered mini-trip}
$W\gets E_j$; $O\gets\langle\ \rangle$; $i\gets u_j$\;
\While{$W\neq\varnothing$}{
 Select $e\in W$ minimizing $c_{i r_e}$\;
 Append $e$ to $O$; remove $e$ from $W$; $i\gets r_e$\;
}
\If{$|O|\geq2$}{
 \Repeat{no strict improvement is found}{
  Evaluate every reversal of a contiguous subsequence of at least two deliveries\;
  Select a candidate with minimum open-path cost\;
  \If{its cost is strictly smaller than that of $O$}{Replace $O$ by this candidate\;}
 }
}
\Return{$(u_j,O)$}\;
\caption{Internal delivery ordering}\label{alg:internal-routing}
\end{algorithm}

The source remains fixed, but the final station may change. The result is a
local optimum for the reversal neighborhood, without considering the next
mini-trip's source or the eventual garage return. Remaining ties in the three
algorithms are resolved by fixed input order.

\subsection{CP variables and notation}
Let $J$ now denote the ordered mini-trips. Their fixed attributes are:
\begin{description}
\item[$u_j,p_j$.] Loading source and product of mini-trip $j$.
\item[$q_j,I_j$.] Total quantity and internal open-path cost.
\item[$e_j,R_j$.] Final delivery location and set of requests served.
\end{description}
For vehicle $k$, define:
\begin{description}
\item[$V_k$.] A sequence variable representing its mini-trips, with distinct
start and end nodes $s_k,t_k$.
\item[$m_{kj}\in\{0,1\}$.] A Boolean variable indicating whether mini-trip
$j$ is required for vehicle $k$, linked to its membership in $V_k$.
\item[$Z_k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}$.] Total transition cost of $V_k$.
\item[$Z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}$.] Total cost over all vehicle sequences.
\end{description}
A required mini-trip must eventually appear, even if its position is not yet
chosen. An unused vehicle has sequence $\langle s_k,t_k\rangle$.

\subsection{Transition-cost matrix}
The cost $C_{ij}$ depends on the kinds of sequence nodes involved:
\begin{equation}
C_{ij}=\begin{cases}
c_{e_i u_j}+h_{p_i p_j}+I_j,&i,j\in J,\ i\neq j,\\
c_{g_k u_j}+h_{b_k p_j}+I_j,&i=s_k,\ j\in J,\\
c_{e_i g_k},&i\in J,\ j=t_k,\\
0,&i=s_k,\ j=t_k,\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cp-transition-matrix}
\end{equation}
The first case connects two mini-trips and includes preparation and execution
of the second one. The second departs from the garage and accounts for the
initial product. The third returns home; the fourth allows an unused vehicle.
Infinity denotes a forbidden transition. Endpoint constraints restrict each
vehicle to its own start and end nodes.

Including $I_j$ on entry counts each internal path once. Because every mini-trip
is performed once, $\sum_jI_j$ is constant during CP optimization. The remaining
decisions affect connecting travel and changeovers.

\subsection{Global constraints and objective}
The model uses named arithmetic and sequence relations.
$\operatorname{Equal}(x,y)$, $\operatorname{LessThan}(x,y)$, and
$\operatorname{LessOrEqual}(x,y)$ mean $x=y$, $x<y$, and $x\leq y$.
$\operatorname{Sum}(X,z)$ constrains $z$ to the sum of the variables in $X$.
These relations are combined with the following sequence constraints.

\begin{description}
\item[Membership and assignment.]
$\operatorname{Member}(V_k,j,m_{kj})$ links the Boolean variable to mini-trip
membership. Exactly one vehicle is required for each mini-trip:
\[
\operatorname{Sum}\bigl((m_{kj})_{k\in K},1\bigr)\qquad(j\in J).
\]
\item[Capacity compatibility.]
A mini-trip that exceeds capacity is excluded:
\[
\operatorname{LessThan}(Q_k,q_j)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Exclude}(V_k,j)
\qquad(k\in K,j\in J).
\]
Exclusion also imposes $\operatorname{Equal}(m_{kj},0)$.
\item[Visit limits.]
Mini-trips serving pieces of one request cannot share a vehicle:
\[
\operatorname{LessOrEqual}\left(\sum_{j\in J:r\in R_j}m_{kj},1\right)
\qquad(k\in K,r\in R).
\]
\item[Vehicle endpoints.]
For $k'\neq k$, impose $\operatorname{Exclude}(V_k,s_{k'})$ and
$\operatorname{Exclude}(V_k,t_{k'})$. Each sequence retains its own endpoints.
\item[Route cost.]
$\operatorname{TransitionCost}(V_k,C,Z_k)$ relates the sequence to its total
transition cost. For a fixed sequence $\langle s_k,j_1,\ldots,j_m,t_k\rangle$,
\[
Z_k=C_{s_kj_1}+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}C_{j_i j_{i+1}}+C_{j_mt_k}.
\]
For an empty sequence, $Z_k=0$.
\item[Total cost.]
$\operatorname{Sum}((Z_k)_{k\in K},Z)$ aggregates the route costs.
The objective is to minimize $Z$.
\end{description}

Here, $\operatorname{Member}$ denotes the mathematical membership relation.
Demand satisfaction, stock usage, and product consistency follow from the
constructed mini-trips. Capacity is checked per mini-trip, since vehicles
reload between trips.

Sequence variables support the membership and insertion relations used in
this model~\cite{delecluse2025sequence}. The transition-cost relation builds
on prior work on sequence constraints~\cite{minicpTransitionCost}. The matrix
$C$ is not assumed to satisfy the triangle inequality; cost filtering and
lower bounds must remain valid without that assumption.

\subsection{Search strategy}
For an unplaced mini-trip $j$, let $F_j$ contain its allowed insertions
$(k,i,l)$, where $i,l$ are consecutive members of $V_k$. Inserting $j$ between
them changes cost by
\[
\operatorname{inc}(j;i,l)=C_{ij}+C_{jl}-C_{il}.
\]
Algorithm~\ref{alg:cp-search} selects a mini-trip with few insertion positions
and explores a minimum-cost insertion first. Each child inherits the current
domains; its changes are undone before the next child is explored.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\SetAlgoLined
\KwData{Current sequence and cost domains; shared best cost $Z^{best}$}
Propagate the constraints and, if an incumbent exists, $\operatorname{LessThan}(Z,Z^{best})$\;
\If{the state is inconsistent}{\Return\;}
\If{all sequences are fixed}{
 Record the solution and update $Z^{best}$\;
 \Return\;
}
Form $F_j$ for each unplaced mini-trip\;
\If{no unplaced mini-trip has an allowed insertion}{\Return\;}
Select $j$ minimizing $|F_j|$ among those with $F_j\neq\varnothing$\;
Select $(k,i,l)\in F_j$ minimizing $\operatorname{inc}(j;i,l)$\;
Recursively explore the child with $\operatorname{Insert}(V_k,i,j)$\;
Recursively explore the child with $\operatorname{NotBetween}(V_k,i,j,l)$\;
\caption{Depth-first insertion search with objective improvement}\label{alg:cp-search}
\end{algorithm}

Initially, there is no incumbent and $Z^{best}=+\infty$. The first child places
$j$ after $i$; the second forbids it between $i$ and $l$, leaving other positions
available. The strategy combines first-fail selection, cost-guided branching,
propagation, and backtracking.

A completed search establishes optimality over the constructed mini-trips,
provided its constraints and bounds are sound. If a computational limit is
reached, the best feasible plan found is retained without claiming optimality.

\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:experimental-setup}
\subsection{Instances}
The study uses 100 synthetic instances with varied configurations. Each specifies
locations, product demands, stocks, vehicle capacities, initial product states,
and changeover costs. Physical station counts are distinguished from request
counts because a station may require several products.

Generation ranges, distributions, random seeds, and feasibility checks must be
recorded to make the collection reproducible. The number and quantity distribution
of constructed mini-trips are also recorded for the CP approach.

\subsection{Evaluation conditions}
Both methods are evaluated on the same data and cost convention. A common
end-to-end computational budget includes preliminary construction, model
preparation, and search. Search time is also reported separately.

The final experimental description records the computing environment, solver
versions, parallelism, stopping criteria, and the chosen $H$ for each MILP run.
Numerical settings are to be completed from the experiment records; they are
not assumptions of the mathematical formulations.

Each plan is independently checked for demand, stock, capacity, product
consistency, visit limits, continuity, and garage endpoints. Both objective
components are recalculated, including initial preparation and final return.
Selected zero-delivery visits in the MILP are identified so that their effect
on the comparison remains explicit.

\subsection{Method comparison}
The evaluation distinguishes construction failure, absence of a feasible
solution within the budget, a feasible solution without an optimality proof,
and completed optimization. Neither a construction failure nor an interrupted
search proves infeasibility of the original problem.

Reported metrics include total cost, travel cost, changeover cost, used vehicles,
loading operations, feasible-solution rate, and time to the best solution.
MILP bounds and optimality gaps accompany the termination status~\cite{gurobiDocs}.
For positive MILP incumbent cost $C_M$, the signed relative difference is
\[
D_C=100\frac{C_{CP}-C_M}{C_M}.
\]
A negative value indicates a cheaper CP plan than the incumbent, not necessarily
than the MILP optimum. If $C_M=0$, the absolute difference is used. The MILP
trip limit and fixed CP mini-trips impose different restrictions, so plan
admissibility is checked before cost differences are interpreted.

\subsection{Effect of changeover costs}
A paired comparison fixes demands, stocks, locations, fleet data, initial
states, and computational budgets. One plan uses the original changeover
matrix $h$; the other uses a zero matrix. Both plans are subsequently evaluated
under the same original matrix $h$.

Let $A$ be the changeover-aware plan and $B$ the travel-only plan. When
$C_h(B)>0$, the observed relative improvement is
\[
G_C=100\frac{C_h(B)-C_h(A)}{C_h(B)}.
\]
If the denominator is zero, report the absolute difference. Travel, changeovers,
assignments, and trip orders are compared separately. Negative improvements
remain possible under limited search effort and are reported.

For CP, the same mini-trips are retained in both conditions. This isolates
changeover effects on assignment and sequencing, without measuring alternative
splitting, source selection, or internal routing.
